# Overview

This portfolio site is both a presentation of work and a designed system in its own right.

I built it to do three things clearly and honestly:

- Explain the impact of my work without requiring insider context
- Show how I think through complex, real-world constraints
- Respect the reader’s time

Most portfolios focus on visual polish or process theater. This one focuses on decision-making — what choices were made, why they were made, and what tradeoffs they involved.

## What this site is (and isn’t)

This site is not a marketing exercise or a fictional redesign playground. Every case study represents real constraints: legacy systems, competing priorities, incomplete data, and stakeholders with different definitions of success.

It is a curated set of six case studies that trace work from early ambiguity to measurable impact. The structure is intentionally consistent so readers can focus on substance rather than learning a new format each time.

## How to read the case studies

Each case study follows the same underlying arc:

Problem framing — what was unclear, risky, or broken

Constraints — technical, organizational, or domain-specific limits

Decisions — what I chose to do (and not do)

Outcomes — impact, learnings, and open questions

I prioritize clarity over completeness. If something is included, it’s because it influenced the outcome. If it’s missing, it’s because it didn’t.

## Design philosophy in practice

A few principles show up repeatedly across the work:

Clarity beats cleverness
Especially in enterprise software, comprehension is a feature.

Constraints are design inputs, not blockers
Technical debt, timelines, and legacy patterns shape solutions — ignoring them doesn’t make them disappear.

Systems matter more than screens
I focus on patterns that scale, not one-off UI moments.

Accessibility is baseline quality
Not a checklist item or a nice-to-have.

These ideas are expanded in more detail in DESIGN_PHILOSOPHY.md, but they’re reflected directly in the work itself.

## Who this documentation is for

This documentation supports different readers with different goals:

Hiring managers can understand scope, ownership, and impact without deep UX context.

Designers can see how research, systems thinking, and tradeoffs connect.

Engineers can understand architectural decisions and collaboration boundaries.

You don’t need to read everything. Each document stands alone, and the README suggests paths depending on what you care about most.

## Why document a portfolio at all?

Complex products deserve explanation — and so do the decisions behind them.

Documenting this site serves the same purpose as the work it presents: making complexity legible, exposing intent, and reducing ambiguity for the people engaging with it.

If you want a guided place to start, see the README.
If you want to understand how the work was shaped, jump into the case studies.

This portfolio is designed for teams building complex, high-stakes software, particularly in mission-driven domains like climate and health.
